translation only), I tried hard to prove it is already known. Searches were actually
run (queries + URLs + findings below). A constraint recorded honestly: **cut-the-knot
pages are unreachable** (gateway denies CONNECT, HTTP 403), so cut-the-knot evidence is
from **search snippets only**.

## Equivalence test used
Two dissections are "the same" if one is a similarity/relabeling of the other: **same
piece shapes, same adjacency, same motions, up to scaling/relabeling** — not merely the
same piece count. Additionally — and decisively here — a dissection is "already
catalogued" if it is a **member of a catalogued parametric family** of dissections (the
family description *is* the catalog entry; every parameter value is covered).

## The decisive collision: C3, the catalogued 2-parameter family

**Query:** `cut-the-knot "Pythagorean theorem" tessellation variant superposition two
lattices how many pieces small square cut` (domain-restricted to cut-the-knot.org), and
`Pythagorean theorem proof tessellation overlay grid offset different dissections`.

**Finding (search snippet of `DoublePythLattice.shtml`):**
> "This proof by tessellation … furnishes a **whole 2-parameter family of various
> dissections** of squares on the legs of a right triangle that combine into a square
> on its hypotenuse. **Perigal's dissection could be obtained from this proof** by
> combining all squares of the same size in natural quartets."

**Interpretation.** K1 is generated by overlaying a `c`-grid on the Pythagorean tiling
(`verify/explore.py`); the grid **offset** is the only free choice. The catalogued
"superposition of two plane tessellations" proof is exactly **the 2-parameter family of
all such offsets**. K1 is the member at the offset that anchors the grid at the
right-angle vertex. Perigal is the member at the big-square-centred offset. **K1 is
therefore a specific point of an explicitly catalogued 2-parameter family** — i.e., it
is already in the catalog, even though it is not the famous Perigal point.

**Verdict for K1: DUPLICATE (family-level).** It is a member of the catalogued
tessellation-superposition family (cut-the-knot `DoublePythLattice.shtml`); it is a
*VARIANT* only relative to the specific Perigal special case. It is **not addable** as
a new dissection.
